What No-Repeat Mode Actually Does

For some draws you want a single number; for others you want a set with no duplicates at all — six unique lottery-style numbers, five different seat numbers, or ten distinct student IDs. Tick the No-repeat box and the generator switches from drawing with replacement to drawing without replacement: once a number comes up it leaves the pool and cannot appear again in the same run.

That rule comes with one condition. The count can never be larger than the size of your range, because you cannot draw ten different numbers from a range of nine. Keep the count at or below (maximum − minimum + 1). With No-repeat switched off the same value may appear more than once, which is exactly what you want for dice-like rolls where duplicates are perfectly normal.

Fair, Private and Instant: What Happens Behind the Button

There is no server in the loop. The generator uses your browser's built-in random number source, draws the values on your own device and prints them straight onto the page, so the range you type is never uploaded, logged or tied to an account. That is also why the result appears the moment you click: nothing is waiting on a network round trip.

Every draw is independent of the one before it. A number that came up a moment ago is exactly as likely as any other on the next click, so long runs and repeats are normal rather than suspicious. That makes the tool a fair way to pick a winner, deal a seat, sample a row or settle an argument.

One caveat worth stating plainly: this is a general-purpose number generator for everyday draws, games and sampling. For passwords, encryption keys or anything security-critical, use a tool built specifically for that job rather than a general number generator.
