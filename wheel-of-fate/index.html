How to Use the Wheel of Fate

Think of a question, press Spin, and read the answer fate lands on. The wheel starts with playful outcomes like Yes, No, Maybe, Try again and Signs point to yes.

Prefer a different kind of decision tool? The Related Tools below link to a name wheel picker, a wheel of colors and other random wheels.

Editing the Fate Outcomes

The wheel opens with a set of playful replies — yes, no, maybe, try again and a few signs-point-to-yes style answers. Replace them with your own and the tool becomes a verdict machine for whatever question you have in mind.

Type the new entries into the box, separated by commas, and the wheel redraws straight away. Some people keep the playful defaults; others load serious answers for a workplace question.

Wheel of Fate vs a Magic 8-Ball

Both give a short, oracular reply to a question you already hold in your head, but they behave differently. A magic 8-ball hides twenty fixed answers inside one plastic shape and you cannot change any of them. The wheel of fate shows every possible answer on its rim, so the whole set of outcomes is visible before you spin.

Seeing the options matters: a cheerful signs point to yes reads differently when an outright no is sitting right beside it.

Party Games and Ice-Breakers

Ask a question out loud, spin, and let the wheel answer for the room. It works as a light-hearted tie-breaker for games, a way to choose the next dare or forfeit, or an ice-breaker that hands a group something to react to.

Because everyone watches the same wheel and the same result, there is nothing to dispute — fate has spoken.

Save Your Favourite Fate Lists

Press Save to keep the current set of answers in your browser, then reload it whenever the same group plays again. You can hold one list of playful replies for parties and another of straight yes or no answers for real decisions, and switch between them in a click.

Questions to Ask the Wheel of Fate

The wheel answers whatever is on your mind, but some questions suit it better than others. Anything you have been going back and forth on works well:

  • Should I send that message tonight or sleep on it?
  • Is this the week to start the project?
  • Do we stay in or go out?
  • Which of these two plans sounds better?

Ask the question out loud, spin, and treat the answer as a nudge rather than an order. The value is in the pause it creates, not in the verdict itself.
