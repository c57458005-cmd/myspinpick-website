Random Group Generator

Split a list of names into random, balanced groups in one click.

How to Randomly Split Names into Groups

Type or paste your list of names into the box (comma separated names), pick how many groups you want, then click "Generate Groups". The tool shuffles the order and distributes names evenly, so every group is as balanced as possible. Results update instantly, right in your browser — nothing is uploaded.

When to Use a Group Generator

  • Classrooms — split students into project teams or activity stations.
  • Team building — create random breakout groups for workshops.
  • Events — assign seats, tables or tournament brackets.
  • Sports — make fair, random teams for practice.

Random Team Generator: Fair Teams in One Click

Set the number of groups to two and the same list becomes a random team generator — half the names go to Team 1 and half to Team 2, with no captain picking favourites and nobody left standing at the side. Shuffling first and then dealing the names out is what keeps the sides even: a list of 23 names splits into 12 and 11, never 15 and 8.

Need more than two sides? Raise the number of groups and the tool doubles as a fixture list for a round-robin, a set of station rotations for a classroom, or breakout rooms for a workshop.

How the Tool Keeps Groups Balanced

Every name is shuffled with a Fisher-Yates shuffle inside your browser, then dealt out one at a time to the groups in turn. Dealing — rather than chopping the list into fixed blocks — is the trick: the next name always goes to whichever group has fewest members, so group sizes differ by at most one. If you need perfectly equal teams and the numbers do not divide evenly, remove one name from the list and generate again.

Same Names, Different Group Sizes

You can split the same list in more than one way, and it is worth trying both before a session starts. Fewer groups means bigger teams, which suits a longer project, a round-robin tournament or a tug-of-war; more groups means smaller teams, which suits pair work, station rotations, reading circles or breakout discussions where everyone should get a turn to talk.

Generate as many times as you need. The names are reshuffled from scratch on every click, so last week's groupings never come back by accident and no student can work out the pattern and swap seats in advance.

Tips for Splitting a Long List

Keep one name per line if your list comes from a spreadsheet or a class register — the box reads commas and new lines equally well. Check the total before you generate: 30 names into 4 groups gives 8, 8, 7 and 7, so one table needs a little more room than the others. And if a split lands badly for any reason, press Generate Groups again; it costs nothing and takes no time.
