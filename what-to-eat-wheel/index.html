How to Use the What to Eat Wheel

Type your meal ideas into the box (comma separated), then click "Spin". The wheel lands on a random dish and shows it as tonight’s pick. Add "Order takeout" or "Cook at home" as options if you like.

Set Up Your Own Dinner List

Start from whatever your household actually eats and type those dishes in, separated by commas. Ten to fifteen meals is plenty — enough variety to feel like a real choice, few enough that the wheel stays readable.

Mix quick weeknight meals with one or two treats so the result stays realistic, and add takeaway as an entry for the nights nobody wants to cook.

Meal Ideas by Cuisine, Mood and Budget

  • Fast and cheap — pasta, omelette, noodles, beans on toast, fried rice
  • Something warming — curry, stew, soup, chilli, roast vegetables
  • Weekend cooking — pizza from scratch, a slow braise, a big breakfast
  • No cooking tonight — takeaway, delivery, or a picnic of whatever is in the fridge

Building the list once saves the same discussion every evening.

Save Your Food Lists for Later

Press Save and the current list is kept in your browser under a name you choose, ready for the next time the question comes up. You can hold separate lists for weekday dinners, weekend cooking and birthdays, and switch between them without retyping anything.

Saved lists live on your own device, so removing them means clearing them here — nothing is copied to a server.

Settling the Dinner Debate

Repeating the same question at the end of a long day is tiring. A spin removes the negotiation and hands the decision to chance, which most households accept far more readily than one person's suggestion.

If the result is genuinely unappealing, spin once more — but the first reaction to a result usually says more than the debate did.

From One Meal to a Whole Week

The same list can plan further ahead than a single dinner. Spin for tonight, press Remove Winner to take that dish out, then spin again for tomorrow — a few presses lay out a week of meals with no repeats.

Households that shop once a week find it quicker to spin the plan first and write the shopping list from the results.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I use the what to eat wheel?

Add the meals you’re considering (comma separated), click "Spin", and the wheel stops on a random dish. You can remove it and spin again until something sounds good.

Is the what to eat wheel free?

Yes. The what to eat wheel is 100% free, runs in your browser, and never requires a sign-up or download.

How many meals should I add to the wheel?

Around ten to fifteen works best. A shorter list spins quickly, keeps every dish visible on the wheel and still feels like a real choice.

Can I save my dinner ideas for next time?

Yes. Use Save to keep the current list in your browser, then load it again with one click. You can hold several lists at once, such as weekday meals and weekend cooking.

Can I use the wheel for lunch or breakfast?

Absolutely. Swap the entries for sandwich fillings, lunch options, breakfast ideas or a whole week of meal planning — the wheel does not mind what the entries are.

What if the wheel picks a meal we do not fancy?

Spin once more, or press Remove Winner to drop that dish before spinning again. You can also edit the list to retire a meal the household has grown tired of.

Can the wheel plan more than one meal?

Yes. Spin for the first meal, press Remove Winner to take it out, then spin again. Repeating that builds a run of different meals for the week.
